## 0. Answer first

**Recommended direction: a new, small brand selling personalised pet portrait prints (monochrome, line or editorial style), printed and shipped from Greece by Gelato. A non-personalised design-print range comes later.**

Why this beats the alternatives:
1. **No inventory and no subscription.** Gelato's free plan, the existing Shopify store and theme infrastructure, €0 stock.
2. **Local production.** Gelato lists Greece as a production country [S], so delivery is domestic, with no customs and fewer damage claims.
3. **Withdrawal-exempt.** Goods "made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised" are excluded from the 14-day right of withdrawal (Directive 2011/83/EU art. 16(c)) [S]. Fashion returns run around 30% [S][?]. This is the single biggest structural margin advantage found.
4. **Light regulation.** Paper prints carry GPSR labelling duties only. Compare cosmetics (Responsible Person, safety report, CPNP) and tech (batteries, WEEE, CE).
5. **An emotional, giftable product with a visual hook.** It suits organic Instagram/TikTok content, which matters because **the model shows paid ads lose money on the first order for every candidate** (§3).
6. **It scales beyond Greece without re-sourcing.** Gelato produces in 32 countries [S].

The honest caveats:
- This is **not** a high-margin goldmine. Base-case contribution is about **€15 per unframed print and €19 per framed one, before ads** [E].
- At realistic Greek Meta costs, a cold-traffic first order loses about €17–64 [E].
- The business only works if it grows through organic content, referrals and gifting seasons, and if the founder's design time per order stays small.
- If the 14-day test does not show organic demand, **stop before spending**.

**What this means**

- **Paid acquisition does not work on the first order.** That holds for every candidate in this study, not just this one.
- The business has to acquire customers through:
  - organic content (the before/after pet-photo → portrait format suits Reels and TikTok);
  - referrals and repeat gifting;
  - seasonal peaks (Christmas, Valentine's, memorials);
  - possibly Etsy, which charges 6.5% + listing fees [S] but brings its own traffic.
- Ads are only worth testing later, retargeting warm audiences, once the conversion rate is known.

## 4. Brand decision (Phase 4)

**C: a new brand for this category, reusing the infrastructure. ILLEGANT stays dormant for a possible fashion line later.**

The reasoning is fit and trust, not aesthetics:
- **Fit.** "ILLEGANT" signals fashion and luxury attitude. A pet-portrait buyer is searching for "πορτρέτο σκύλου" or "pet portrait". The name supplies neither relevance nor search value.
- **Trust.** 73% of Greek shoppers trust e-shops backed by a known brand [S]. Neither name is known. A clear, category-descriptive name lowers the trust gap more than a fashion name pointed at a different product.
- **Risk containment.** If the test fails, ILLEGANT remains untouched for its original purpose.

**Why not B (evolve ILLEGANT into "ILLEGANT Pets" or "ILLEGANT Home")?** It keeps the mismatch and dilutes a fashion brand that has not launched yet.

**What is reused:**
- the Shopify store;
- the monochrome theme work (the editorial, black-and-white look suits line-art portraits);
- the notify-me and coming-soon system;
- the policies drafts.

Choosing the new name is the founder's decision. I did not check trademarks or domains, and buying a domain needs approval.

## 6. Why not the founder's original plan (fashion dropshipping)

Here the brief asked for protection rather than reassurance:
- **Margins.** A POD premium tee leaves about €5 per order after a ~30% return rate [E][S?].
- **Fees.** EU dropship platforms charge €30–69 a month before the first sale [S][?], larger than the stated budget.
- **Premium positioning.** It would need samples, white-label labels and returns handling the budget cannot support.

Fashion can come back later as ILLEGANT, once there is capital and a proven audience.
